// Services / DeFi

DeFi protocols ship faster than auditors can keep up.

DeFi composability is the source of both its value and its largest attack surface. Every new protocol inherits the security assumptions of the protocols it integrates with. Every state change ripples through positions held by users you've never seen. The hardest bugs aren't in your contracts. They're in the interactions between your contracts and the ones you depend on.

Existing solutions don't keep pace. A single complex DeFi audit runs $75K to $250K and takes weeks or months. By the time a report ships, the code has moved on.

// Attack surface

Where the bugs hide.

01

Cross-contract reentrancy

State assumptions that hold within a single function break when execution flow returns through composable callbacks. The hardest reentrancy bugs span contract boundaries.

02

Oracle manipulation

Flash loans, price feed staleness, and TWAP edge cases turn read-only values into attack vectors. Most oracles look correct in isolation and fail under adversarial conditions.

03

Accounting precision

Rounding errors, share/asset drift, and fixed-point math bugs that drain protocols slowly or all at once. Off-by-one in the wrong place is a million-dollar bug.

04

Composability assumptions

What happens when a protocol you integrate with pauses, upgrades, or itself gets exploited. Your security depends on assumptions you didn't write.

05

Governance and access control

Privileged functions, time-locked parameter changes, and emergency procedures that don't behave the way the documentation says they do.

06

MEV and ordering

Frontrunning, sandwich attacks, and value extracted by sequencers and searchers in ways the protocol didn't model.
