// Services / Formal Verification

Prove what matters. Find what doesn't.

Some bugs are hard to find by review or by fuzzing: the ones that only appear in specific state combinations, the ones that violate an invariant nobody thought to write down, the ones in math-heavy contracts where precision matters more than execution path coverage. Formal verification is how serious protocols prove these properties hold for every possible input, not just the ones that came up in testing.

The cost of formal methods has historically been the bottleneck: writing specs, mapping invariants to provers, debugging counter-examples. The protocols that invest in it anyway are the ones that don't get exploited the way DeFi did in 2022 and 2023.

Properties worth proving.

01

Accounting invariants

Total supply, share-to-asset relationships, and value-preservation properties that must hold across every state transition. Proof beats coverage when money is on the line.

02

Access control

Who can call what under which conditions, expressed as provable properties rather than tested code paths. Tests confirm what you remembered to check; proofs confirm everything else too.

03

Math-heavy contracts

Pricing curves, AMM math, and compound interest where edge cases hide in bounds. Proving the math means catching what fuzzing won't reach.

04

State machines

Lifecycle transitions in protocols where invalid sequences must be impossible, not just unhandled. Liveness and safety as proven properties.

05

Integration contracts

Properties about how your protocol behaves when called by arbitrary external code, invariants that can't be confirmed by reviewing your contracts in isolation.

06

Cryptographic protocols

Signature schemes, hash chains, and Merkle proofs where correctness depends on assumptions about underlying primitives.
